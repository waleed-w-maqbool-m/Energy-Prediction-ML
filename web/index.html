PJM East electricity demand, forecast a week ahead

A gradient-boosting model that combines temperature, calendar and recent demand to predict daily grid load, with prediction intervals calibrated on held-out data. Trained on 2002–2018 data for the eastern part of the largest U.S. grid operator. The model runs in your browser.

Intervals are calibrated on a two-year window before the hold-out and then checked on the hold-out. They are somewhat too narrow when the weather is assumed rather than known, which is why that case is reported separately.

Stability across years

Walk-forward backtest: for each year the model is retrained on everything before it, then scored one day ahead on that year.

Which inputs matter

Permutation importance on the hold-out: how much worse the forecast gets when one input is randomly shuffled. Recent demand sets the level; temperature explains the day-to-day swings that yesterday's demand cannot.

Demand against temperature

Demand rises in both directions away from a mild day: heating in winter, air conditioning in summer. Heating and cooling degree days (distance from 18.3 °C) give the model this V-shape directly.
